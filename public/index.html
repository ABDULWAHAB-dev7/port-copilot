How it works

Each sentence in a draft has to point at a line in the file.

The drafter reads only the documents attached to the chosen shipment. A sentence that cannot be matched to a line is rejected and listed, so you can see what is missing.

  1. Step 01

    Pick the shipment and the question.

    Each shipment carries its own set of documents, such as the invoice, bill of lading, packing list and certificate of origin.

  2. Step 02

    Every sentence is tied to a document line.

    The quoted text must appear exactly on the cited line. Numbers and codes are compared as well.

  3. Step 03

    Unsupported sentences are rejected and listed.

    Rejected sentences and the documents still needed appear beneath the draft with the reason given.

  4. Step 04

    You approve before anything is copied.

    Editing the draft clears the approval. Copying is the only action the tool takes, and it sends nothing.

            Limits

            What this tool does not do.

            A short list, written before you rely on anything here.

            It does not file anything.

            Drafts and checks are for a licensed professional to review. Nothing reaches an agency or a terminal from this page.

            It reads text, not scans.

            An upload is read as text: a .txt or .csv file, a pasted block, or a PDF that carries a text layer. A scanned page has no text to read, so its text has to be pasted. The charge reader still expects the sample invoice layout and refuses another one with a message, rather than guessing at it.

            The samples are synthetic; your uploads are not stored.

            The bundled shipments, questions, tariff card and invoices are made up for demonstration. Anything you upload is used for that one request: the application keeps no database, writes nothing to disk and holds no session. The terms and privacy notice set out the rest.
